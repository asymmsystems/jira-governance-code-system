# LedgerSeal: USP, compliance and UX research brief

- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Question:** why would a regulated Jira team pick LedgerSeal over native Jira controls and the Marketplace approval, e-signature and audit-trail apps? Which compliance statements can the app honestly make? Which UX delivers the result?
- **Code reviewed:** `apps/ledgerseal` in the working tree at repo HEAD `423b9b2`. Every LedgerSeal file has the same md5 as the audit snapshot (`docs/sdlc/portfolio-audit-2026-10-31/snapshot-md5.txt`), so every LS finding is still open as written (section 2). `git status` shows no change under `apps/ledgerseal`.
- **This is a product analysis, not legal advice.** It tests marketing claims against regulation text. A customer's validation, procedures and auditor decide compliance.
- **Evidence grades** (audit scheme): Observed-code, Observed-test, Observed-live, Reported, Inferred. No live-site call ran in Part A.
- **Source IDs:** REG (regulations, standards, guidance), NJ (native Jira and Atlassian programs), JAC (jira.atlassian.com tickets), FG (Forge platform), MP (Marketplace apps), T (tests run in this research). Section 9 lists every source.
- **Quote caveat:** most Atlassian, eCFR, FDA and NIST quotes were downloaded and extracted verbatim. Pages marked "[fetch tool summarised]" in section 9 came through a summarising fetch tool. Check those quotes against the URL before any external use.

## 1. Executive summary

1. No candidate USP passes all five conditions today, because LS-01 and LS-02 block every integrity claim (section 5; T-02 A1, A2).
2. One position passes four of five conditions and needs the backlog for the fifth: **approval-bound, auditor-verifiable change evidence**. It combines a field diff with who and when, a reseal by a different approver that keeps the earlier record, an Ed25519-signed attestation, and an open verifier. No checked app documents any of the last three (MP section 6, MP-70, MP-71).
3. Native Jira is the main substitute, not the Marketplace. A status lock exists (`jira.issue.editable`, NJ-01 to NJ-05), but with gaps: admin-backed app bypass (NJ-06), no team-managed support (JAC-05), no field-level security (JAC-01, 589 votes).
4. "Enforces 21 CFR Part 11 electronic signatures" is Not true. The app meets no Part 11 signature clause in full. 11.200 needs two identification components, and the app accepts any typed text (REG-24, T-02 A6). Four outside sources say software cannot be Part 11 compliant on its own (REG-107 to REG-109, REG-94).
5. The plain SHA-256 digest is not enough evidence against an adversary (REG-98). NIST names signed hashes with asymmetric keys (REG-102, REG-106).
6. Demand for a hash seal is not proven. No auditor or buyer statement was found either way (REG section 3). Rival seal apps have 1 to 686 installs (MP-01, MP-51, MP-54).
7. Price: $1.50 per user at 100 users equals the top of the checked range ($0.50 to $1.50, MP pricing note). Willingness to pay is Unknown.
8. Drop first: the Part 11 claim, the SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / SOX keywords, and the "Cloud Fortified" badge, which Atlassian is phasing out by the end of 2026 (NJ-36).
9. Build first: B-01 to B-10 in `backlog.md`, starting with the approver policy (LS-01) and the tamper record that survives a reseal (LS-02).

Expected USPs and the five conditions:
- **Three strongest, in order:** U5 (the combination), U3 (signed attestation), U4 (open verifier). U1 and U2 are necessary parts, but each fails "defensible" alone.
- **Refute attempts (round 3 in `round-log.md`):**
  - U1: native approvals re-open only on status re-entry, not on an edit (MP-72). Two small apps compare approved and current values (MP-70, MP-71). The U1 gap now holds only for "with who and when".
  - U2: JSM can exclude listed users from approving (NJ-15). This covers first approval, not re-approval after a change. Gap holds; defensibility fails.
  - U3: SealDoc uses HMAC (MP-54), and Signoff uses an unnamed fingerprint (MP-70). Neither offers public-key verification. Gap holds.
  - U4: Vellum says "a third party can recompute the chain without the app" (MP-60). It covers history, not approval seals. Gap holds narrowly.

### 6.3 Wording the app can support

**Today (the reviewed code):**
- "LedgerSeal stores a SHA-256 digest of most issue fields when a user seals an issue. It shows which of those fields changed since, and it can block a workflow transition until the issue is sealed and unchanged."
- Required caveat today: "Any user who can view the issue can seal or reseal it." No Part 11, SOC 2, ISO 27001 or SOX wording is supported today.

**After B-01 to B-09 (Part B must prove each item):**
- "Each seal records the signer's Jira name, the time and the meaning of the signature (supports 21 CFR 11.50(a))."
- "Only users in your approver policy can seal, reseal or revoke. A user who changed the issue cannot approve that change (supports authority checks, 21 CFR 11.10(g))."
- "LedgerSeal can block a workflow transition until the issue is sealed and unchanged (supports operational sequencing checks, 21 CFR 11.10(f))."
- "LedgerSeal keeps every seal, change incident and reseal in a hash-chained history. A reseal does not hide an earlier change."
- "Seal records are signed with Ed25519. An auditor can check them with our published public key. The key is held by the vendor, so the signature proves that LedgerSeal wrote the record, not that the vendor could not."
- "LedgerSeal can provide evidence for the approval step of a change management control, such as SOC 2 CC8.1 or ISO 27001 A.8.32. Your auditor decides whether it is sufficient."
- "LedgerSeal does not provide 21 CFR Part 11 electronic signatures by itself. It does not perform the two-component signing in 11.200. Compliance depends on your validation and procedures."
