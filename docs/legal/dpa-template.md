## Background

A. Asymm Systems publishes four apps for Jira Cloud on the Atlassian Marketplace: CascadePlan,
   LedgerSeal, DefinitionGuard and FourRealities (each an **App**, together the **Apps**).

B. The Customer uses the Apps under the Atlassian Marketplace standard end user agreement, the
   Bonterms Standard End User Agreement, Version 1.0
   (<https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/marketplace/end-user-agreement-v1>), with Asymm Systems
   as the Provider (the **End User Agreement**). The Asymm Systems terms page
   (<https://asymm.systems/terms>) states this.

C. When the Customer uses the Apps, Asymm Systems processes personal data on behalf of the
   Customer. The parties make this agreement to meet Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (the
   **GDPR**).

## 1. Definitions and order of precedence

1.1 Words defined in the GDPR have the same meaning here: "personal data", "processing",
"controller", "processor", "data subject", "personal data breach", "supervisory authority".

1.2 **Customer Personal Data** means the personal data described in Annex I that the Apps
process for the Customer.

1.3 **Forge** means Atlassian Forge, the platform on which Atlassian hosts and runs the Apps.

1.4 **SCCs** means the standard contractual clauses for the transfer of personal data to third
countries in the Annex to Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021.

1.5 If this agreement and the End User Agreement conflict on the processing of Customer
Personal Data, this agreement applies. If this agreement and the SCCs conflict, the SCCs apply.

## 2. Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose

2.1 **Subject matter.** The processing of Customer Personal Data by the Apps, as the Customer uses
them on its Atlassian cloud site.

2.2 **Duration.** This agreement applies for as long as any App is installed on the Customer's
site, and after that until Customer Personal Data is deleted as section 11 states.

2.3 **Nature.** The Apps run only on Forge. Asymm Systems operates no external servers for them.
The Apps call only Atlassian's own APIs: the Customer's Jira and Confluence, and Atlassian's
personal data reporting service. The Apps store their data in Forge key-value storage for the
Customer's site and, where Annex I says so, in issue properties of the Customer's Jira issues.

2.4 **Purpose.** Only to provide the functions of each App that the Customer uses, as Annex I
describes, and to meet Atlassian's personal data reporting rules for apps.

## 3. Instructions (Article 28(3)(a))

3.1 Asymm Systems processes Customer Personal Data only on the Customer's documented
instructions. This agreement, the End User Agreement, and the Customer's configuration and use of
the Apps are the Customer's complete instructions at the date of this agreement. Other
instructions must be in writing and must be consistent with the functions of the Apps.

3.2 This includes transfers of personal data to a third country. Section 7 states the transfers
that the Customer instructs. Asymm Systems makes no other transfer, unless Union or Member State
law to which it is subject requires it. In that case Asymm Systems tells the Customer of that legal
requirement before processing, unless that law forbids this on important grounds of public
interest.

3.3 Asymm Systems tells the Customer at once if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes the
GDPR or other Union or Member State data protection law.

## 4. Confidentiality (Article 28(3)(b))

4.1 Asymm Systems makes sure that each person it authorises to process Customer Personal Data is
bound by a duty of confidentiality, by contract or by law.

4.2 Access is limited to the people whose work needs it. Asymm Systems reviews access each quarter
and removes what is no longer needed (security policy, <https://asymm.systems/security>,
section 2).

## 5. Security of processing (Article 28(3)(c) and Article 32)

5.1 Asymm Systems implements and keeps the technical and organisational measures in Annex II.

5.2 Asymm Systems may change a measure, but a change must not lower the overall level of
security of Customer Personal Data.

## 6. Sub-processors (Article 28(2), 28(3)(d) and 28(4))

6.1 The Customer gives Asymm Systems general written authorisation to use the sub-processors in
Annex III. At the date of this agreement the only sub-processor is Atlassian, as the provider of

6.3 Asymm Systems makes sure that each sub-processor is bound by data protection obligations that
give at least the protection that Article 28(3) requires. Asymm Systems remains fully liable to the
Customer for the performance of each sub-processor's obligations, as Article 28(4) requires.

## 8. Assistance with data subject requests (Article 28(3)(e))

8.1 Taking into account the nature of the processing, Asymm Systems helps the Customer, by
appropriate technical and organisational measures where possible, to answer requests from data
subjects to exercise their rights under Chapter III of the GDPR.

8.2 The Apps do this in part by themselves. Each App reports the Atlassian account ids it holds to
Atlassian's personal data reporting service, and, when Atlassian reports an account as closed,
removes the account id from its records or deletes the link from the pseudonym to the account id
(Annex I, "Erasure of closed accounts").

8.3 For any other request, the Customer writes to privacy@asymm.systems. Asymm Systems answers
within 30 days (privacy policy, "Privacy contact").

8.4 If a data subject sends a request about Customer Personal Data to Asymm Systems directly, Asymm
Systems passes it to the Customer without undue delay and does not answer it itself, unless the
Customer instructs it to.

## 9. Assistance with security, breach, DPIA and prior consultation (Article 28(3)(f))

9.1 Taking into account the nature of the processing and the information it has, Asymm Systems
helps the Customer to meet its obligations under Articles 32 to 36 of the GDPR: security of
processing, notice of personal data breaches, data protection impact assessments and prior
consultation of the supervisory authority.

9.2 For a data protection impact assessment, Asymm Systems gives the Customer, on request, the
information it has on the processing: this agreement and its Annexes, the privacy policy, the
security policy, and its answers to the Atlassian Marketplace security questionnaire for each App.

## 10. Personal data breaches

10.1 Asymm Systems tells the Customer of a personal data breach of Customer Personal Data without
undue delay, and within 72 hours of confirming the breach. This matches the security policy,
<https://asymm.systems/security>, section 6.

10.2 The notice gives, as far as Asymm Systems knows them at the time: the nature of the breach,
including the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned; the name
and contact details of the contact point; the likely consequences; and the measures taken or
proposed to deal with the breach and to reduce its possible harm. Where Asymm Systems cannot give
all of this at once, it gives the information in phases without further undue delay.

10.3 Asymm Systems follows the steps of its incident response (detect, contain, assess, notify,
fix, review) in the security policy, section 6. It also tells Atlassian, as Atlassian's app
security incident management guidelines for Marketplace Partners require.

10.4 The Customer decides whether to notify the supervisory authority and the data subjects.

## 11. Deletion and return at the end (Article 28(3)(g))

11.1 When the Customer uninstalls an App, Atlassian keeps that App's Forge storage for 28 days and
then deletes it (privacy policy). Before it uninstalls, the Customer can keep what it needs from the
App's own views and records `[CONFIRM: which App functions export data, for example LedgerSeal's

11.2 Issue properties that an App set on the Customer's Jira issues (Annex I) stay with the issues
until the Customer deletes the issues or the properties. They are part of the Customer's own Jira
data, under the Customer's control.

11.3 At the Customer's choice, Asymm Systems deletes or returns Customer Personal Data that it
still holds, within `[60]` days of the Customer's written request, and confirms the deletion in
writing. This does not apply where Union or Member State law requires Asymm Systems to keep the
data.

`[CONFIRM: that Asymm Systems has a way to delete or return the Forge storage of one installed

## 12. Information and audits (Article 28(3)(h))

12.1 Asymm Systems makes available to the Customer all information needed to show that it meets
Article 28, and allows and contributes to audits, including inspections, by the Customer or by an
auditor that the Customer appoints.

12.2 First, Asymm Systems answers the Customer's written questions and security questionnaires,
and gives the documents in section 9.2.

12.3 If those answers are not enough, the Customer may carry out an audit, by giving at least
`[30]` days' written notice, not more than once in any 12 months, unless a supervisory authority
requires it or a personal data breach of Customer Personal Data has occurred. The auditor must be
bound by confidentiality. The audit takes place during normal business hours in Sri Lanka and
must not give access to other customers' data.

12.4 Each party bears its own costs of an audit.

12.5 Asymm Systems cannot give audit rights over Atlassian's systems. For the Forge platform, Asymm
Systems gives the Customer the information that Atlassian publishes about its own security and
compliance.

## 13. Liability

13.2 Nothing in this agreement limits a party's liability to data subjects under Article 82 of the
GDPR, or any liability that the SCCs do not allow to be limited.

13.3 This agreement contains no late fee, no interest, no penalty charge and no liquidated damages,
and no amount payable under it grows because of delay.

## Annex I: Description of the processing

### I.1 For all Apps

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Data subjects | Users of the Customer's Atlassian cloud site whose actions an App records, and people named in issue content that an App stores (LedgerSeal only) |
| Personal data | Atlassian account ids, and pseudonyms of account ids (per App below). No App stores email addresses. |
| Issue data | Issue data held in Forge storage, per App below. LedgerSeal stores the sealed issue content as Jira holds it, and FourRealities and DefinitionGuard store free text that users write. This content can name people. |
| Special categories (Article 9) | None intended. The Apps do not ask for them. They can appear only if users write them into issue content or free text. |
| Frequency | Continuous, while an App is installed |
| Nature of processing | Read from the Customer's Jira (and, for FourRealities, Confluence) through Atlassian's APIs; store in Forge key-value storage for the Customer's site and in issue properties; show in the App's views; report account ids to Atlassian's personal data reporting service |
| Where it is stored | Forge storage, which Atlassian hosts in the data residency location of the Customer's site; issue properties in the Customer's Jira |
| Out of residency scope | Forge function logs. They hold failure locations, HTTP status codes or error type names, counts, and for some Apps issue keys or project keys. The questionnaire answers state that they hold no account id, name, email or issue text. |
| Erasure of closed accounts | Each App reports its stored account ids to Atlassian's personal data reporting service. CascadePlan reports each id at most once every 7 days, from an hourly job. LedgerSeal, DefinitionGuard and FourRealities run a weekly job. When Atlassian reports an account as closed, the App removes the id, or deletes the link from the pseudonym to the id. |
| Retention | As long as the App is installed; then as section 11 states |

## Annex II: Technical and organisational measures

The measures in II.1 are the ones that the security policy (<https://asymm.systems/security>)
publishes. The measures in II.2 are in the App code and manifests. The measures in II.3 are
Atlassian's.

### II.1 Company measures (security policy)

| Area | Measure | Security policy section |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts | Multi-factor authentication is on for every company account: the GitHub organisation, email, Atlassian and Cloudflare. | 2 |
| Passwords | Passwords are kept in a password manager. Each password is strong and used for one account only. | 2 |
| Endpoints | The Linux workstations run ClamAV. | 2 |
| Patching | Operating systems and software are kept up to date with security updates. | 2 |
| Least privilege | Each person gets only the access their work needs. Access is reviewed each quarter, and what is no longer needed is removed. | 2 |
| Change review | Every change is made on a branch, and an independent review of the change is done before it is merged. | 3 |
| Continuous integration | Automated tests and a dependency audit run in CI. For the Apps the audit is `npm audit`; a high or critical finding fails the build. | 3 |
| Secure coding | Changes are reviewed against the OWASP Top 10 risks. | 3 |
| Secrets | Secrets are never stored in code. The Apps keep their secrets as encrypted Forge environment variables. | 3 |
| Signing keys | LedgerSeal and DefinitionGuard sign some records with Ed25519. Each key has a key id, and each signed record names its key id. Each key is rotated every 12 months, and at once if a private key may be exposed. The first scheduled rotation is in December 2026. Public keys, and retired public keys, stay published on the signing keys page. | 4 |
| Vulnerability reports | Reports go to security@asymm.systems; acknowledgement within 72 hours; a fix for a critical vulnerability is targeted within 30 days; for the Apps, Atlassian's Security Bug Fix Policy for Marketplace apps applies where its time frames are shorter. | 5 |
| Incident response | Detect, contain, assess, notify (affected customers, and Atlassian for the Apps), fix, review. Affected customers are told without undue delay, and within 72 hours of confirming a breach of their personal data. | 6 |

### II.2 App measures (code and manifests)

| Area | Measure | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| No egress | No App manifest declares `permissions.external` or `remotes`. The Apps call only the Customer's Jira (and, for FourRealities, Confluence) and Atlassian's personal data reporting service. | `apps/*/manifest.yml`; questionnaire question 6 for each App |
| Runtime | Each App runs on Forge, runtime `nodejs22.x`. | `apps/*/manifest.yml` |
| Storage | Each App stores its data in Forge key-value storage (`@forge/kvs`) for the Customer's site. | `apps/*/src`, imports of `@forge/kvs` |
| Least privilege scopes | Each App asks only for the scopes it uses. LedgerSeal and DefinitionGuard write to Jira only through the granular scope `write:issue.property:jira`. | `apps/*/manifest.yml`; questionnaire question 7 |
| Access control | The Apps act on Jira data within the user's own Jira permissions: calls for a user run as that user, and each call as the App on an action that needs a user permission comes after a permission check, as the user, with Jira's permissions REST API. Admin functions check the global Administer Jira permission as the user. | questionnaire questions 1 and 3 for each App `[CONFIRM: the LedgerSeal and FourRealities answers describe permission-check branches. Both are merged to master (696c763, 67b6fd6); confirm that the production versions include them before this is offered]` |
| Pseudonymisation | LedgerSeal names people in its records by a keyed hash of the account id. DefinitionGuard stores people as random pseudonyms, with the account id only in one link per person. | privacy policy; listing files |
| Data minimisation | No App stores email addresses. FourRealities stores no names; it reads them from Jira when it shows them. | privacy policy |
| Erasure of closed accounts | Personal data reporting and erasure, as Annex I.1 describes. | `apps/*/manifest.yml` scheduled triggers; privacy policy |
| Logs | Backend log lines hold no account id, name, email, issue text, token or key. | questionnaire question 8 for each App |
| Input validation | The issue and project come from the Forge context, not from the request. Paths to Jira are built with the `route` tag of `@forge/api`, which encodes them. | questionnaire question 9 for each App |
| Tamper evidence | LedgerSeal and DefinitionGuard keep hash-chained histories, so changes to stored entries can be detected. This does not stop someone who can rewrite all of an App's stored data, including the vendor, from writing a new consistent chain. | compliance page; listing files |
