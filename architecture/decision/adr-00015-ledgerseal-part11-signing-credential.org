#+TITLE: ADR-015: LedgerSeal Signing Credential for 21 CFR 11.200 and 11.300
#+DATE: 2026-11-08
#+STATUS: Proposed

* Status
[PROPOSED: 2026-11-08, awaiting the owner's approval. Nothing is built.]

The owner's process needs this ADR, the spec delta (=docs/spec/ledgerseal-spec-v4-draft.md=,
section 2) and the high-level design (same file, section 2.6) approved before any code. The
related ADRs are ADR-016 (approval routes) and ADR-017 (signing on the transition screen).

* Context
** The gap
LedgerSeal records who sealed an issue, when, and with which meaning. The signer is the Atlassian
account that called the resolver (=context.accountId=, spec v3 invariant 2). The signer does not
enter anything that proves, at the moment of signing, that they are the account holder. The
listing and the compliance page say so: "LedgerSeal does not re-authenticate the signer and does
not perform two-component signing" (11.200 and 11.300 "Not met",
=src/main/jbake/content/product/jira-governance/compliance.html= in
=asymmsystems/asymm-corporate-code-pfw=, origin/develop =7fcd97a=).

The gap research (=docs/launch/research/dc-migration-gap-2026-11.md=, section 4, ranks 1 and 3)
names this as the first wedge: Deviniti "Electronic Signature for Jira" has no cloud version, and
Appfire Electronic Signatures cloud checks a credential before the transition while LedgerSeal
does not.

** What the regulation asks (eCFR, Title 21 up to date as of 2026-11-05, quoted verbatim)
- 11.100(a): "Each electronic signature shall be unique to one individual and shall not be reused
  by, or reassigned to, anyone else."
- 11.100(b): "Before an organization establishes, assigns, certifies, or otherwise sanctions an
  individual's electronic signature, or any element of such electronic signature, the organization
  shall verify the identity of the individual."
- 11.200(a)(1): "Employ at least two distinct identification components such as an identification
  code and password."
  - (i): "When an individual executes a series of signings during a single, continuous period of
    controlled system access, the first signing shall be executed using all electronic signature
    components; subsequent signings shall be executed using at least one electronic signature
    component that is only executable by, and designed to be used only by, the individual."
  - (ii): "When an individual executes one or more signings not performed during a single,
    continuous period of controlled system access, each signing shall be executed using all of the
    electronic signature components."
- 11.200(a)(2) "Be used only by their genuine owners"; (a)(3) "Be administered and executed to
  ensure that attempted use of an individual's electronic signature by anyone other than its
  genuine owner requires collaboration of two or more individuals."
- 11.300(a) uniqueness of each combined identification code and password; (b) issuances
  "periodically checked, recalled, or revised (e.g., to cover such events as password aging)";
  (c) "loss management procedures to electronically deauthorize lost, stolen, missing, or otherwise
  potentially compromised tokens, cards, and other devices that bear or generate identification
  code or password information, and to issue temporary or permanent replacements using suitable,
  rigorous controls"; (d) "transaction safeguards to prevent unauthorized use of passwords and/or
  identification codes, and to detect and report in an immediate and urgent manner any attempts at
  their unauthorized use to the system security unit, and, as appropriate, to organizational
  management"; (e) "Initial and periodic testing of devices, such as tokens or cards, that bear or
  generate identification code or password information".

FDA still enforces these sections. The 2003 guidance "Part 11, Electronic Records; Electronic
Signatures - Scope and Application" limits enforcement discretion to validation, audit trail,
record retention and record copying, and says: "we intend to enforce ... requirements related to
electronic signatures (e.g., §§ 11.50, 11.70, 11.100, 11.200, and 11.300)."

FDA does not prescribe a method. The October 2024 guidance "Electronic Systems, Electronic Records,
and Electronic Signatures in Clinical Investigations: Questions and Answers" (Q24): "part 11
regulations do not specify a particular method to create a valid electronic signature", and (Q28)
"FDA does not certify electronic systems and methods used to obtain electronic signatures."

The 1997 preamble (62 FR 13430) explains three points this design relies on:
- comment 125: the identification code need not be secret: "uniqueness of the identification code
  when combined with another electronic signature component, which may not be unique (such as a
  password), makes the combination unique";
- comment 124: the "continuous session" relaxation needs controls such as "automatic inactivity
  disconnect measures that would de-log the first individual";
- comments 132 and 138: 11.300(c) and (e) are meant "to cover all types of devices that contain or
  generate identification code or password information".

** What Forge allows (developer.atlassian.com, read 2026-11-08)
- *No re-authentication.* No Forge or Jira API checks an Atlassian user's password, forces a fresh
  login or offers step-up. Two community questions about it (2021, 2022) have no Atlassian answer.
  The resolver context gives =accountId=, =cloudId=, =license= and similar fields, and no session
  id or login time.
- *Atlassian passwords are off limits.* Marketplace security requirements, Privacy 15: "An
  application must not collect or store credentials belonging to Atlassian user accounts such as
  user passwords or user API tokens." The note says it "does not prohibit apps from storing
  credentials used to access non-Atlassian applications."
- *Secret storage.* =kvs.setSecret=, =kvs.getSecret=, =kvs.deleteSecret= store values "in a secure,
  encrypted manner"; they "can't be queried"; values up to 128 KiB; =storage:app= scope. The storage
  page: "only use encrypted environment variables and kvs.setSecret to store secrets or
  credentials in your app."
- *Crypto.* The Forge Node.js runtime supports Node.js 22 and 24 and "any built-in" module.
  =crypto.createHmac=, =randomBytes=, =timingSafeEqual= and =scrypt= exist in Node 22.
  =crypto.argon2= was "Added in: v24.7.0", so Argon2 needs the =nodejs24.x= runtime (LedgerSeal runs
  =nodejs22.x=). Resolvers have 25 seconds; functions 128 to 1,024 MB (default 512 MB).
- *External identity providers.* Forge external OAuth needs =remotes= and
  =permissions.external.fetch.backend=. "Runs on Atlassian" says: "Your app must not egress data,
  with the exception of egress for analytics purposes." So a step-up through an outside IdP ends
  the "Runs on Atlassian" badge. Whether =prompt=login= or =max_age= pass through
  =queryParameters= is not documented.
- *Alerts without egress.* =POST /rest/api/3/issue/{issueIdOrKey}/notify= "Creates an email
  notification for an issue". Scopes: classic =write:jira-work=, granular =send:notification:jira=
  (Beta). It answers 403 when "outgoing emails are disabled". Recipients: users, groups, watchers,
  reporter, assignee, voters. No official page says it works as the app. No API lets an app write
  Jira's audit log (=/rest/api/3/auditing/record= is GET only).
- *How the cloud competitor does it.* Appfire Electronic Signatures cloud: "Unlike Electronic
  Signatures for Jira DC, which uses passwords, Electronic Signatures for Jira Cloud uses personal
  PIN codes for authentication. These PINs are delivered via email each time you need to sign".
  The user signs in an "E-Signatures" window on the issue view, then transitions.

* Options considered
** (a) A LedgerSeal signing credential: signing ID plus a TOTP authenticator
A signing administrator issues a credential to a named user: a signing ID and a one-time enrolment
code. The user enrols an authenticator app (RFC 6238 TOTP) in their own Jira session. The seed is
kept with =kvs.setSecret=. At each signing the user types the signing ID and the current six-digit
code; the resolver verifies both.
- 11.200(a)(1): met. Component 1 is the signing ID (an identification code, comment 125). Component
  2 is a code made by a device only the user holds. Both are executed at every signing.
- 11.300: (a) met (unique IDs, 160-bit random seeds); (b) met (expiry, then re-enrolment with a new
  seed); (c) met technically, and it is the case the rule names: a device that "generate[s] ...
  password information"; (d) prevention met (lockout, one use per code), detection and report met
  through recorded alerts and Jira email; (e) initial test at enrolment, a test at every use, and a
  "test my authenticator" action.
- Threats: a colleague at an unlocked desk needs the phone too; a stolen phone needs the Atlassian
  session too; the issuer never sees the seed. Residual: a real-time relay of a code inside its 30
  to 60 seconds; malware on the phone; app code (the vendor) can read the seed.
- Usability: about 10 seconds more per signing; one enrolment per person per period.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.8 accepts a code when "Copy and paste can be relied on to avoid
  transcription". Desktop authenticators and password managers with TOTP support pasting. The
  form keeps one paste-able field and no timer of its own.
- Recovery: a lost phone needs a new issuance by a signing administrator (support work).
- Runs on Atlassian: yes. HMAC-SHA-1 and random bytes are in =node:crypto=. No egress.

** (b) A LedgerSeal signing passphrase, hashed with a memory-hard function
Signing ID plus a passphrase the user sets, stored as an =scrypt= hash (Node 22) or Argon2id (needs
=nodejs24.x=).
- 11.200(a)(1): met. It is the literal example, "an identification code and password".
- 11.300: (a), (b) met; (c) a passphrase has no device, so only "potentially compromised" applies;
  (d) lockout met, but sharing a passphrase is easy and leaves no trace; (e) not applicable.
- Threats: shoulder surfing, sharing, reuse of a known password, offline guessing by anyone who can
  run code as the app. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (section 3.1.1.2) asks at least eight characters for a
  password used inside multi-factor authentication, salted and hashed.
- Usability: simplest; no second device. Accessibility: a memory test, acceptable under SC 3.3.8
  only when password managers can fill it ("Mechanism").
- Recovery: an administrator sets a temporary passphrase that must be changed at first use; the
  administrator knows it for that window, which weakens 11.200(a)(3).
- Runs on Atlassian: yes.

** (c) The Atlassian session plus one separate component
The Atlassian login counts as the first signing, and each later signing asks only for a PIN or a
TOTP code (11.200(a)(1)(i), preamble comment 124).
- The app cannot see the login, the session start, its length or an inactivity logout. There is no
  session id in the Forge context. So LedgerSeal cannot show that a signing happened "during a
  single, continuous period of controlled system access", and it cannot apply the de-log control
  FDA describes. Without that, (a)(1)(ii) applies, and one component per signing does not meet it.
- A customer whose identity provider forces short sessions and inactivity logout might validate
  this reading. LedgerSeal would not claim it.
- Variant (c2): force a fresh login at an outside IdP through Forge external OAuth. It needs
  egress, so the app loses "Runs on Atlassian"; =prompt=login= is not documented; and each customer
  needs its own IdP set-up. Rejected for now (see the table below).

* Decision (recommended)
Adopt option (a). Details:

1. *Credential.* One LedgerSeal signing credential per person: a signing ID plus a TOTP
   authenticator (RFC 6238: HMAC-SHA-1 for authenticator compatibility, 6 digits, 30-second step,
   160-bit seed from =crypto.randomBytes=). The code of the current step and of the one step before
   is accepted (RFC 6238 section 5.2: "at most one time step is allowed as the network delay").
   Each step is accepted once per credential ("The verifier MUST NOT accept the second attempt of
   the OTP").
2. *All components at every signing.* LedgerSeal treats every signing as outside a continuous
   session (11.200(a)(1)(ii)): the user types the signing ID and the code each time. The Atlassian
   login remains the access control of 11.10(d), not a signature component.
3. *What counts as a signing.* In "Part 11 signing" mode every seal, reseal (approval of changes),
   revoke, stage signature (ADR-016) and rejection needs the credential. The mode is off by default
   and set by a Jira administrator; while it is off, LedgerSeal behaves as in spec v3.
4. *Issuance and enrolment (11.100(b), 11.200(a)(3)).* A Jira administrator chooses the signing
   administrator groups (a new party role =LS_SIGNING_ADMIN=). A signing administrator, after
   checking identity under the customer's procedure, issues a credential to a named user: a
   signing ID and a one-time enrolment code (valid 24 hours, shown once). The user enters it in
   their own session and sees the seed once (QR code and text); then confirms two consecutive codes
   (initial device test). A signing administrator cannot issue a credential to themself. The record
   keeps the issuer and the time.
5. *Uniqueness and no reuse (11.100(a), 11.300(a)).* A signing ID is unique on the site and is
   never given to another person, even after the account is closed (a keyed hash of the ID is kept
   for ever; see decision 11).
6. *Safeguards (11.300(d)).* Five failed attempts in a row lock the credential until a signing
   administrator unlocks it with a reason. Every failure, lock, unlock, issuance, enrolment, loss
   report and revocation is an event in a hash-chained credential history. Failures and locks are
   alerts: shown on the admin page and sent by Jira email through the notify API on an "alert
   issue" that the administrator names. When Jira cannot send the email, the alert stays on the
   admin page and the panel of signing administrators says so.
7. *Periodic revision (11.300(b)).* A credential expires after a period the administrator sets
   (default 365 days, 30 to 730). Renewal enrols a new seed under the same signing ID.
8. *Loss (11.300(c)).* The user can report the device lost or compromised; the credential stops at
   once. A signing administrator can revoke a credential. A replacement is a new issuance.
9. *Device testing (11.300(e)).* At enrolment (two codes), at every use, and on demand ("Test my
   authenticator", which signs nothing and is recorded).
10. *What a signature records.* Each signature row adds =data.signing=: the method
    (=LS_ID_TOTP_1=), the credential id (random, not an account id), the credential revision and
    the number of components executed. It never holds the code, the seed or the signing ID text.
11. *Personal data (ADR-011).* The signing ID is kept in clear only while the account is active. On
    closure the credential is revoked, its seed deleted, and only a keyed hash of the signing ID is
    kept, so the ID can never be reused. Events name the party token only.
12. *Declaration.* Part 11 mode uses a new declaration version that states that the electronic
    signature is the legally binding equivalent of a handwritten signature. The owner writes the
    wording (open question Q-03).

* What remains the customer's responsibility
- Identity checks before issuance (11.100(b)) and the letter of non-repudiation to FDA (11.100(c)).
- Validation of LedgerSeal in their process (11.10(a)), training (11.10(i)), written policies that
  hold signers accountable (11.10(j)), and the decision that this design meets their predicate
  rules.
- Access control to Jira itself (11.10(d)): SSO, multi-factor login, session and idle timeouts.
- Policies against sharing devices (11.200(a)(2)); keeping signing administrators apart from
  Atlassian organisation administrators who can take over a user's account (11.200(a)(3)).
- The expiry period, loss reporting, periodic device tests and the response to alerts (11.300(b) to
  (e)); keeping Jira outgoing email on; the choice of authenticator apps and phone clock sync.

* Consequences
** Positive
- The 11.200(a)(1) and 11.300 rows can move from "Not met" to "Partly met" (draft spec section 6),
  without leaving Forge or adding egress.
- The cloud signing step is closer to Data Center apps than an emailed PIN, and is stated in the
  same honest terms.
** Negative
- New scope for alerts: =send:notification:jira= (Beta) or =write:jira-work=; site admins consent
  on upgrade (open question Q-02).
- The app now holds per-user secrets. The security questionnaire answer 13 ("one secret") changes;
  answer 11 (Atlassian credentials) stays "No".
- The vendor can deploy code that reads the seeds and signs as a user. This is the same trust limit
  as the attestation key, and the listing must say it.
- Support work for lost phones; one more admin role.
- A QR code needs either a small encoder in the repository or a dependency (open question Q-04).

* Considered and excluded
| Option | Why excluded |
|---|---|
| Ask for the Atlassian password | Forbidden: Marketplace security requirements, Privacy 15 |
| Step-up at the customer's IdP (option c2) | Needs egress and ends "Runs on Atlassian"; =prompt=login= is not documented for Forge providers; per-customer set-up. Reconsider if a customer asks and accepts the badge loss |
| Emailed one-time PIN (the Appfire cloud method) | Needs a write or notify scope on every signing; email delay; a shared or delegated mailbox is not "only executable by ... the individual"; email is not a device the app can deauthorise |
| WebAuthn or passkeys | UI Kit renders in Jira's page with no browser API access; Custom UI runs in an iframe whose origin and permissions policy the app does not control. Not verified as possible |
| Biometrics (11.200(b)) | No sensor access in Forge |
| Passphrase only (option b) | Weaker against sharing, no device to deauthorise, and Argon2 needs a runtime change. Kept as a fallback if the owner rejects a second device (open question Q-01) |
| The "continuous session" relaxation (11.200(a)(1)(i)) | The app cannot see the session, so it cannot show the condition |
| Signing ID plus passphrase plus TOTP | Three components; more friction than the rule needs. Can be added later as an option |
